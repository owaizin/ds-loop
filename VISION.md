# Design System Loop — the vision

**Your design-system team, in one install.**

Most teams building a product don't have a design-system team. The ones that do
can't hire enough of one. Design System Loop is that team: a set of agents and
tools, packaged as one kit, that does what an elite design-system team or a
specialist agency would do for you.

## What happens when you start it

1. **It investigates.** It reads your codebase, your tokens, your components,
   your Storybook and your docs, and establishes facts. Every fact points to a
   file and a line. Nothing is guessed.
2. **It consults.** It comes back like an agency would, with a presentation,
   not a log:
   - **The problem.** What is actually hurting you, in plain words.
   - **Before.** Your system as it is today, with its architecture drawn out.
   - **Ideal.** What the best teams do in your situation.
   - **After.** Where this plan takes you, and the architecture it proposes.
   - **The plan.** Ordered steps, what each one changes, and how you'll know it
     worked.
3. **It does the work.** With your approval, its agents build foundations,
   migrate components, write docs, set up Storybook and fix drift.
4. **It keeps the system healthy.** A live dashboard, hooked to the real code,
   shows where you stand and how that's changing. Checks run on every edit and
   in CI, so the system doesn't slide back.

## The standard it holds you to

Elite design-system teams: Primer, Polaris, Carbon, Atlassian, Spectrum, Radix.
How they start a system, maintain it, migrate to it, document it and use
Storybook. Those practices are written into the kit's playbooks, checked by its
tools and benchmarked against real open-source systems. The benchmark is never
a grade someone invented.

## The soul: the experience of using it

- **Clear words at every layer people see.** The engine and agents keep precise,
  stable terms so they can reason reliably. Anything a person reads, including
  terminal output, reports and the agent's voice, uses clear names with a short
  description underneath. Nobody has to learn our vocabulary to understand
  their own system.
- **Show, don't dump.** Understanding happens in a visual report and a
  dashboard: diagrams, before and after, and clear next steps. Someone who only
  uses the terminal still gets a short, readable summary instead of a wall of
  findings. Presentations are consulting-grade, the kind you'd forward to your
  CEO.
- **It leads, and it never assumes.** It investigates first so it doesn't ask
  what it could have found out. Then it offers a choice: "Want me to ask you a
  few questions, or go ahead on what I found?" The interview, when there is
  one, is short and simple. Each question is explained with an example and
  asked one at a time, so it never feels like homework.
- **It's honest.** It says what it checked and what it couldn't check. Silence
  is never passed off as a clean bill of health.

## Who it's for

- **Teams without a design-system team** (most startups): it acts as that
  team.
- **Teams with one**: it adds a team's worth of hands and a consistent
  standard.

## What stays true underneath

- **Facts before advice.** A deterministic engine (no AI, no network) produces
  the evidence. The agents reason on top of it and never instead of it.
- **Your team decides.** Agents recommend and deliver. The team approves
  shared-system decisions.
- **Local and private.** Your code doesn't leave your machine because of this
  kit.
- **Open.** MIT. Given to teams, and sold as a service by the people who
  built it.

## Success looks like

A stranger installs it, types one command, and within half an hour is looking
at an agency-grade report of their design system and a plan they would have
paid a consultant for. Three months later, the dashboard shows the plan worked.
